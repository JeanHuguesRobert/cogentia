# Research anchors — administrative burden and institutional friction

This note records the main research traditions to be cited and deepened in later versions of `administrative_burden_and_exemplar_tests.md`.

## Administrative burden

Core distinction: learning costs, compliance costs, and psychological costs experienced by people interacting with the state. The modern literature treats administrative burden as a policy outcome, not merely an implementation inconvenience.

## Red tape

Barry Bozeman's work distinguishes burdensome rules from rules whose burden is insufficiently justified by their contribution to legitimate organizational purposes. This is directly relevant to proportionality tests in institutional-friction cases.

## Street-level bureaucracy

Michael Lipsky's framework shows that frontline implementation is itself a form of policymaking: the real policy experienced by users may materially differ from formal rules or public statements.

## French sociology of the guichet

Vincent Dubois studies the concrete production of public policy at administrative counters and through interactions with users.

## Non-take-up / non-recours

Philippe Warin and ODENORE document cases in which formally available rights or public services are not effectively used or accessible, including because of how the offer and procedures are designed.

## Sludge

Behavioral public-policy research studies avoidable procedural friction: repeated forms, excessive steps, difficult cancellation or enrollment, documentary demands, and other small barriers that can materially change behavior and access.

## Institutional decoupling

Meyer and Rowan describe divergence between formal organizational structures and actual operational practices. Formal structures may provide legitimacy even where day-to-day activity is only loosely coupled to them.

## Organizational hypocrisy

Nils Brunsson analyzes systematic divergence among organizational talk, decisions, and actions, including cases where contradictory pressures make such divergence structurally useful without requiring simple bad faith.

## Cogentia extension

Cogentia adds three operational commitments:

1. preserve a strict separation between attributable facts and interpretations;
2. measure burden and its distribution through `Follow the Burden`;
3. construct an **Exemplar Test** where possible, turning an asserted alternative into an executable counterfactual whose cost and effects can be compared with the existing arrangement.

This note is a research pointer, not a substitute for a properly sourced bibliography. Later revisions should attach primary or authoritative sources for each tradition.
