# Janus — Cognitive Gatekeeper

## Abstract

**Janus** is proposed as the bidirectional, non-monotonic transition governor of the Cognitive Packet Graph.

Its two faces govern two different but coupled promotions:

```text
PAST
trace
→ candidate relation
→ qualified Cognitive Packet relation
→ knowledge

FUTURE
Cognitive Packet
→ candidate continuation
→ qualified continuation
→ progressively embodied test or action
```

Janus does not create Reality, perform synthesis, compute every evaluation, authorize every act, or replace specialized models.

It governs **promotion across thresholds**.

Its retrospective face, **Janus.Past**, qualifies how traces, observations, representations and outcomes may be attributed to Cognitive Packets and assimilated into a revisable representation of the Realized.

Its prospective face, **Janus.Future**, qualifies how Cognitive Packets may branch toward candidate continuations and how far those continuations should advance from abstract possibility toward simulation, synthetic consequence, Reality Probe and material incarnation.

The key invariant is:

> **A transition in the Cognitive Packet Graph must not be promoted beyond what evidence, constraints, mandate and the current encounter with Reality justify.**

Janus is not proposed as an isolated invention. It is a proposed point of synthesis between established and emerging traditions including truth maintenance, belief revision, provenance, belief-state estimation, world models, planning, uncertainty gating, long-horizon agent control, permission governance and traceable memory.

The working hypothesis is that **Cognitive Packets provide a shared governed object on which these traditions can compose**.

## 1. Why Janus

Janus is the Roman figure of gates, passages and transitions, conventionally represented with two faces.

The metaphor is useful here because cognitive work is situated between two open boundaries:

```text
an incompletely reconstructed past
              ↓
           PRESENT
              ↓
an unactualized and incompletely known future
```

The choice of name is functional rather than theological or mythological.

It also turns out not to be unique. Several contemporary AI systems independently use the name *Janus* for mechanisms involving memory control, permission decisions, past precedents, future simulation or anticipatory safety.

Under the Corpus rule **Friends before competitors**, this convergence should first be treated as evidence of conceptual kinship rather than as a naming collision to defend against.

A research question follows:

> **Are these independent Janus systems manifestations of a more general architectural need: governing transitions between what a system retains from the past and what it permits itself to do toward the future?**

## 2. Core distinction: qualification is not production

Janus is a **governor**, not the universal producer of the values it consumes.

A specialized system may estimate:

```text
evidence strength
uncertainty
risk
cost
potentiality
predicted consequence
capability availability
expected information gain
```

Janus may use these assessments without having to compute them all.

A useful architecture is:

```text
provenance / belief revision
world models / simulation
Potentics
risk estimators
capability models
uncertainty estimators
        ↓
     assessments
        ↓
       JANUS
        ↓
qualified transition
```

Therefore:

> **Janus does not necessarily compute evidence strength, potentiality, expected value or risk. It governs promotion using such assessments under declared policy.**

This boundary prevents Janus from becoming an artificial super-component combining epistemology, planning, risk, scheduling, authorization and execution.

## 3. Janus and the Spirit of Synthesis

The Corpus already contains an older conceptual structure that Janus must not replace.

In the Potentics formulation:

```text
The Possible
→ Exploration
→ Synthesis
→ Actualization
→ New capabilities
→ New accessible possibles
```

The **Spirit of Synthesis** names the hypothesized generative dynamic through which previously separate elements, observations, capabilities or partial solutions combine so that new possibles become accessible and sometimes actualized.

Its earlier symbolic formulation is:

```text
FATHER
The Possible
possibles not yet incarnated

SON
The Realized
possibles that have become incarnated

HOLY SPIRIT
Spirit of Synthesis
the generative passage of combination and actualization
```

This is a structural and personal interpretation, not a claim about Christian dogma.

Janus occupies a different role.

```text
Spirit of Synthesis
= generative combination and actualization

Janus
= governance of transitions around that process
```

A compact formulation is:

> **The Spirit of Synthesis makes new configurations possible and actual; Janus governs the thresholds by which they are cognitively promoted and later assimilated.**

## 4. The larger cycle

The resulting architecture is recursive:

```text
                    THE POSSIBLE
                         │
                Machine à Explorer
                         │
              candidate continuations
                         │
                         ▼
                   JANUS.FUTURE
                         │
               qualified transition
                         │
              degree of embodiment
                         │
                         ▼
              SPIRIT OF SYNTHESIS
                         │
                   actualization
                         │
                         ▼
                    THE REALIZED
                         │
                       traces
                         │
                         ▼
                    JANUS.PAST
                         │
          reconstruction / assimilation
                         │
                knowledge / capability
                         │
                         └───────────────→
                       new accessible possibles
```

The loop matters more than any individual component.

What has been realized changes the capability landscape, and therefore changes which parts of The Possible become accessible next.

## 5. Cognitive Packet Graph

Let:

\[
G = (P,T,R)
\]

where:

- \(P\) is the set of Cognitive Packets;
- \(T\) is the set of cognitively relevant traces;
- \(R\) is a set of typed, qualified and revisable relations.

Examples include:

```text
generated_by
evidence_for
evidence_against
outcome_of
continuation_of
triggered
constrained
modified
resolved
superseded
observed_during
derived_from
fork_of
merged_into
revived_from
```

A relation has more than structural type.

It may also carry:

```text
epistemic status
temporal orientation
provenance
confidence or evidence class
revision conditions
unresolved residue
```

Janus governs promotion inside this graph.

## 6. Canonical does not mean eternally true

A canonical Cognitive Packet representation is the best currently governed representation of:

```text
identity
state
lineage
causal frontier
known constraints
unresolved residue
continuations
relevant references
```

Canonicalization is therefore operational, not metaphysical.

```text
canonical
≠ historical omniscience
≠ irreversible truth
```

A canonical state remains revisable as additional traces appear.

This makes Janus inherently **non-monotonic**.

A relation may move through:

```text
candidate
→ accepted
→ challenged
→ revised
→ superseded
```

Likewise:

```text
dead-end
→ revived
```

The graph may become better informed without pretending that earlier uncertainty never existed.

## 7. Janus.Past

Janus.Past governs retrospective promotion.

A minimal abstraction is:

\[
J_{past}(T,\{P_i\},E,C)
\rightarrow
Q
\]

where:

- \(T\) is a trace or trace set;
- \(\{P_i\}\) are candidate Cognitive Packets;
- \(E\) is available evidence and provenance;
- \(C\) is the relevant constraint and policy context;
- \(Q\) is a qualified transition result.

Possible outcomes include:

```text
accepted
candidate
ambiguous
rejected
unresolved
```

Janus.Past should conceptually separate at least three gates.

### 7.1 Identity Gate

Does the trace concern this Packet at all?

```text
Trace T
→ concerns CP-A ?
→ concerns CP-B ?
→ both ?
→ unresolved ?
```

### 7.2 Relation Gate

If attribution exists, what relation is supported?

```text
T generated_by P
T evidence_for P
T evidence_against P
T outcome_of P
T modified P
...
```

### 7.3 Canonicalization Gate

Is the evidence sufficient to promote this relation into the current canonical representation?

This is distinct from mere storage.

A trace can be retained without being canonically assimilated.

## 8. Attribution is not truth

A key distinction is:

```text
trace attribution
≠
truth of the trace content
```

For example:

```text
"This document unquestionably belongs to CP-42."
```

may be strongly supported while:

```text
"The factual assertion contained in the document is true."
```

remains contested.

Therefore two relations may coexist:

```text
Trace → Packet
Content → Claim / State
```

with different epistemic statuses.

Janus.Past must not silently transform reliable provenance into reliable truth.

## 9. Latent Cognitive Packets

A Cognitive Packet may become operationally identifiable before a complete canonical representation exists.

Working definition:

> **A latent Cognitive Packet is an identifiable unit of continuing cognitive work whose logical identity is inferable from distributed traces although no sufficiently complete canonical Packet representation has yet been materialized.**

This preserves:

```text
Packet identity
≠
Packet representation
```

and:

```text
reconstruction
≠
historical truth
```

A Packet may therefore be reconstructed progressively from email, commits, issues, documents, observations, conversations or other traces.

## 10. Unresolved residue and the Black boundary

Not every trace-to-Packet relation can immediately be resolved.

The architecture must represent:

```text
UnknownRelation(T)
```

without silently converting it into:

```text
NoRelation(T)
```

Earlier discussion used the expression **Black Cognitive Packet** for this fallback.

The term should remain deliberately provisional.

A safer interpretation is a special unresolved sentinel or boundary state:

```yaml
kind: unresolved-packet-relation
semantics: fallback
```

rather than automatically treating Black as an ordinary Cognitive Packet satisfying all Packet closure conditions.

The rule remains:

> **Attach the unknown; do not explain it away.**

Explicit competing hypotheses should be preserved before collapsing their remainder into Black:

```text
Trace T
 ├─ CP-A under H1
 ├─ CP-B under H2
 └─ unresolved residue
```

## 11. Unknown past and unactualized future are not the same

The two faces of Janus are symmetric only in a limited structural sense.

The past boundary concerns epistemic incompleteness about events that already produced effects.

The future boundary additionally concerns **non-actuality**.

Therefore:

\[
UnknownPast \neq UnactualizedFuture
\]

Both may appear dark from the present, but for different reasons.

The architecture should preserve that distinction.

## 12. Janus.Future

Janus.Future governs prospective promotion.

A minimal abstraction is:

\[
J_{future}(P,\{N_i\},K,B)
\rightarrow Q
\]

where:

- \(P\) is the current Cognitive Packet;
- \(\{N_i\}\) are candidate continuations;
- \(K\) contains relevant knowledge and assessments;
- \(B\) contains applicable bounds, budgets and constraints;
- \(Q\) is a qualified prospective result.

Candidate statuses include:

```text
open
promising
probe-first
defer
blocked
dead-end-candidate
closed-by-evidence
```

Three questions must remain distinct.

### Possibility Gate

Is the continuation excluded by known Reality or hard constraints?

### Potentiality Gate

If possible, how promising is it under present conditions?

### Actionability Gate

If promising, is there currently enough capability, evidence, mandate and resource to advance it?

Thus:

```text
possible
≠ promising
≠ actionable now
```

## 13. Dead ends are not impossibilities

A future branch may currently be a dead end without being impossible.

\[
DeadEnd(Map_t)
\not\Rightarrow
Impossible(Reality)
\]

A useful distinction is:

```text
dead-end-candidate
  no useful continuation currently known

closed-by-evidence
  excluded under explicit sufficiently supported constraints
```

Even `closed-by-evidence` should remain scoped to the evidence and regime that justify closure.

A changed environment, new capability, new knowledge or new synthesis may justify revival.

## 14. Structural grammar

The following relations can occur retrospectively or prospectively:

```text
continuation
fork
merge
supersede
dead-end
revive
```

Their structural type should remain separate from epistemic status.

For example:

```text
fork
status: observed
```

and:

```text
fork
status: possible
```

describe structurally similar relations with different temporal and epistemic status.

## 15. Simulation is not Reality

A future simulation remains a model-produced artifact.

Therefore:

```text
simulated future
≠ Future
```

and:

```text
simulation available
≠ foresight useful
```

A world model may generate accurate-looking trajectories while still degrading decision quality if the agent:

```text
simulates at the wrong moment
trusts the wrong rollout
misreads the simulation
fails to relate it to the decision
cannot later account for its quality
```

This motivates a stronger requirement than mere access to simulation.

## 16. Synthetic Skin in the Game

The Corpus already defines **Synthetic Skin in the Game** through the Rossignol principle.

A software agent does not possess biological skin in the game.

It does not thirst, bleed, lose social standing intrinsically, or experience its own death.

Therefore a synthetic architecture can impose limited consequential constraints such as:

```text
real finite resource consumption
bounded inference or exploration budget
authority reduction after repeated failure
persistent prediction ledger
loss of trust weight
mandatory human review
non-resettable causal trace
```

The essential property is:

> **A failure cannot be unilaterally erased by restarting the process that caused it.**

Synthetic Skin is not equivalent to genuine human or biological Skin in the Game.

\[
SyntheticSkin \neq LivingSkin
\]

But it can create useful selection pressure where otherwise a reasoning system could generate unlimited consequence-free speculation.

## 17. Foresight must become answerable

A simulation becomes cognitively stronger when it creates a later obligation to compare prediction with Reality.

A minimal loop is:

```text
prediction
→ decision consequence
→ Reality
→ observed result
→ comparison
→ learning
```

Janus.Future may therefore promote some prospective claims into identifiable prediction-bearing Packets.

Example:

```yaml
prediction:
  packet: cp:57
  expected_outcome: X
  alternatives:
    - Y
    - Z
  decision_consequence: "promote branch A"
  verification_condition: "observable event E"
  revision_condition: "outcome differs materially from X"
```

When Reality returns:

```yaml
reality_return:
  prediction: cp:57
  observed_outcome: Y
  consequence: "preferred branch failed"
  learning: "reweight model M"
```

Janus.Past then assimilates the result.

This produces an important invariant:

> **Janus.Future makes commitments; Janus.Past makes them answerable.**

## 18. Prediction quality is not enough

A prediction may be correct by chance and useless for the decision.

A prediction may be wrong yet useful because it exposed a decisive risk or led to a discriminating probe.

Therefore the architecture should distinguish:

```text
prediction accuracy
epistemic calibration
decision utility
information gain
resource cost
capability gain
```

Synthetic Skin should not become a primitive punishment mechanism.

The target is **learning and selection**, not retribution.

## 19. The Embodiment Ladder

Not every prospective question requires the same encounter with Reality.

A working ladder is:

```text
E0 — Abstract
reasoning, hypothesis, conceptual branch

E1 — Simulated
world-model rollout, counterfactual, sandbox

E2 — Synthetic stake
budget, authority, reputation, prediction ledger,
persistent accountability

E3 — Material probe
real system, bounded resources, operational experiment

E4 — Human / living stake
real consequences borne by people, animals or other living subjects
```

This is not a quality ranking.

It is an **Exposure and incarnation scale**.

Higher levels may supply stronger discrimination while simultaneously creating stronger ethical and governance obligations.

## 20. Least Sufficient Embodiment Principle

Measured Risk already replaces universal risk minimization with the **smallest sufficient risk**.

Janus extends this into prospective epistemology:

> **Promote a continuation toward the least embodied encounter sufficient to discriminate it.**

Formally, the objective is not:

\[
min(Reality)
\]

but something closer to:

\[
min(Embodiment)
\]

subject to:

\[
DiscriminatingPower \ge D_{required}
\]

and all applicable:

```text
Mandate
rights
Exposure limits
recovery conditions
responsibility boundaries
```

The operational sequence becomes:

```text
think
→ simulate if sufficient
→ add synthetic stake if needed
→ perform bounded Reality Probe if needed
→ expose living subjects only when the question cannot responsibly
  be answered at a lower level of embodiment
```

Compression:

> **Use as little Reality as sufficient — but enough Reality to let Reality answer.**

## 21. Real stakes strengthen the duty of care

The Rossignol principle already establishes:

> **The more real the skin in the game, the stronger the duty of care must become.**

Therefore escalation toward real human or biological consequence does not grant permission to experiment.

It does the opposite: it raises the required standard of:

```text
mandate
justification
protection
monitoring
stop conditions
recovery
compensation
human responsibility
```

This prevents the epistemic value of real stakes from becoming a justification for manufacturing avoidable suffering.

## 22. The cost of the possible: philosophical ancestry

Janus also belongs to an older problem: the relation between possibility, actualization, suffering and the claim to optimize a world.

### 22.1 Leibniz

Leibniz's doctrine of the **best of all possible worlds** does not mean the world containing the least local suffering.

Its structure permits the idea that a locally costly or harmful state may coexist with a globally preferable world.

Janus does not adopt Leibnizian theodicy.

The useful ancestry is the more abstract problem:

```text
global good
≠ simple minimization of every local cost
```

### 22.2 Voltaire and the Pangloss failure

*Candide* famously attacks philosophical optimism through repeated encounters between theory and suffering.

In Janus terms, Pangloss illustrates a powerful anti-pattern:

```text
Reality contradicts the Map
→ contradiction is rationalized
→ confidence does not decrease
→ canonical representation does not revise
```

Call this the **Pangloss Loop**.

A system in which no conceivable Reality trace can lower confidence does not possess an effective Janus.Past gate.

### 22.3 Huxley and the Huxley failure

The French title of Aldous Huxley's *Brave New World*, *Le Meilleur des mondes*, creates an obvious resonance with the Leibnizian tradition, regardless of whether a specific authorial intention behind the translation is established.

Huxley's dystopia supplies the opposite warning:

```text
reduce pain
reduce uncertainty
reduce instability
        ↓
suppress divergence
suppress autonomy
suppress meaningful possibility
        ↓
declare the system optimized
```

Call this **Huxley Optimization**.

It warns against:

\[
GoodWorld = min(Suffering)
\]

when the minimization destroys effective freedom and the accessible possibility space.

## 23. Suffering, capability and effective freedom

The Corpus should therefore avoid an unqualified principle of suffering minimization.

A better working principle is:

> **Among continuations compatible with rights, mandate and sufficient effective freedom, seek the required learning, capability or value with the least irreducible suffering and irreversible loss.**

This resembles an optimization problem only schematically.

Relevant dimensions are not necessarily reducible to one scalar:

```text
learning
capability
effective freedom
optionality
suffering
damage
Exposure
irreversible residue
distribution of loss
```

Critically:

> **The benefit of one Principal must not silently cancel the loss imposed on another.**

## 24. Janus, Potentics and effective freedom

Potentics distinguishes:

```text
The Possible
possibility
potentiality
actualization
capability
```

The Machine à Explorer discovers and develops candidate paths.

The Machine à Rendre Capable transforms selected possibilities into effective, situated capacity.

This suggests a useful interpretation:

> **Effective freedom may be approximated by the topology of continuations that are actually accessible from the current state.**

Janus.Future participates by preventing premature closure while also preventing unjustified promotion.

The rule is:

> **A Gate regulates promotion, not existence.**

Therefore:

```text
not promoted
≠ impossible

deferred
≠ deleted

dead-end
≠ eternally closed
```

## 25. Interroger le Réel

When evidence is insufficient, Janus should not force a model-only completion.

The Corpus rule remains:

> **Do not ask the model to complete Reality when Reality itself can be asked to answer.**

Therefore:

```text
uncertainty
→ candidate Reality Probe
→ Janus.Future
→ least sufficient embodiment
→ probe
→ trace
→ Janus.Past
→ revision
```

A Reality Probe is thus a prospective Cognitive Packet whose result becomes retrospective evidence.

## 26. Machine à Explorer

The Machine à Explorer and Janus should remain distinct.

```text
Machine à Explorer
→ generate / discover continuations

Janus
→ qualify their promotion

Reality
→ discriminate

Janus.Past
→ assimilate the return
```

Janus should not suppress generativity merely because most generated possibilities will not be promoted.

Exploration benefits from broad possibility generation.

Promotion benefits from stronger governance.

## 27. Memory and Mnemes

A Mneme is not a Cognitive Packet.

A trace retained as governed memory may contribute to several Packets, and a Packet may depend on many Mnemes.

```text
many Mnemes → one Packet
one Mneme → several Packets
```

Janus.Past therefore sits downstream from memory retention but upstream from stronger canonical claims.

A future implementation could extend the Corpus Sleep Cycle:

```text
raw traces
→ candidate Mnemes
→ consolidation
→ Janus.Past
→ Packet reconstruction
```

while preserving the fact that memory selection and Packet reconstruction are distinct acts.

## 28. Locality

Janus should respect the Minimum Sufficient Locality principle.

A Cognitive Packet need not contain every piece of memory or evidence it depends on.

It can preserve:

```text
identity
state
frontier
lineage
unresolved residue
candidate continuations
references
```

while large or governed evidence remains local.

Therefore:

> **The Packet travels; governed memory may remain local.**

Janus qualification should operate through bounded projections and references rather than uncontrolled replication.

## 29. Authority boundary

Janus qualification must never be confused with authorization.

The existing distinction remains:

```text
Intent
≠ Mandate
≠ Capability
≠ Authorization
```

A transition may be:

```text
epistemically qualified
potentially valuable
technically feasible
```

and still not be authorized.

Therefore:

```text
Janus
→ qualified transition

Mandate / authorization
→ permitted Act
```

This separation is especially important at higher Embodiment levels.

## 30. Friends of Janus

The state of the art should be approached under the Corpus rule:

> **Look for friends before competitors. Seek kinship, reuse and composition before differentiation. Preserve what remains different.**

### 30.1 Truth Maintenance and belief revision

Truth Maintenance Systems and Assumption-Based Truth Maintenance Systems provide mature ancestors for:

```text
explicit reasons
dependency tracking
contradiction
revision
competing assumption sets
```

They are natural friends of Janus.Past.

The question is not how to replace them, but how their mature machinery can inform or implement retrospective Packet qualification.

### 30.2 Belief states and POMDPs

Belief-state methods provide a family of mechanisms for turning incomplete observations into a maintained representation of latent state.

They are natural friends of the transition:

```text
history
→ present uncertain state
```

A belief engine may therefore become one evidence provider to Janus rather than an alternative architecture.

### 30.3 World models and foresight

World models generate possible future consequences of actions.

They are natural friends of Janus.Future.

Their output can be treated as:

```text
candidate evidence about futures
```

rather than Future itself.

### 30.4 Uncertainty and safety gates

Uncertainty gating, anticipatory safety and execution-readiness mechanisms can become specialized providers or sub-gates.

For example:

```text
safety estimator
→ safety assessment
→ Janus.Future
```

rather than:

```text
Janus = safety estimator
```

### 30.5 Permission and human-control systems

Permission-management research is a friend of the transition from:

```text
qualified
→ authorized
```

It can help determine how machine qualification should be exposed to a human Principal without generating permission fatigue or fake human control.

### 30.6 Friends named Janus

Several contemporary systems independently use *Janus* for memory update control, permission management, safety foresight or past/future decision guardrails.

This should be studied as a possible convergence rather than primarily treated as a naming problem.

A future comparison should ask:

```text
What function is common?
What can be reused?
What can be composed?
What remains materially different?
```

## 31. Generic Janus interface

A deliberately abstract representation is:

\[
J :
CandidateTransition
\times Evidence
\times Assessments
\times Constraints
\rightarrow QualifiedTransition
\]

A minimal record might be:

```yaml
janus_decision:
  direction: past | future
  subject: <packet-or-transition>
  structural_relation: <type>
  decision: <status>
  basis:
    evidence: []
    assessments: []
    constraints: []
  epistemic_status: <status>
  unresolved: []
  revision_conditions: []
```

For Janus.Future:

```yaml
janus_decision:
  direction: future
  subject: cp:42
  structural_relation: continuation
  decision: probe-first
  assessments:
    possibility: open
    potentiality: promising
    uncertainty: high
  embodiment:
    current: simulated
    required_next: synthetic
  revision_conditions:
    - "synthetic test discriminates branch A from B"
```

## 32. Invariants

### J1 — No silent promotion

No relation advances to a stronger epistemic or operational status without an explicit basis.

### J2 — Unknown must remain representable

Lack of resolution must not require fabrication.

### J3 — Unknown is not absent

\[
UnknownRelation(T) \neq NoRelation(T)
\]

### J4 — Canonical remains revisable

Canonical state is governed current knowledge, not eternal truth.

### J5 — Dead-end is not impossible

A presently inaccessible continuation must not be silently erased from The Possible.

### J6 — Qualification is not authorization

Janus cannot manufacture Mandate.

### J7 — Reality may overrule

Model consensus does not outrank relevant external evidence.

### J8 — Structural and epistemic status remain separate

`fork`, `merge`, `continuation` and `supersede` do not themselves state truth or certainty.

### J9 — Preserve residue

Unassimilated evidence and competing hypotheses are information.

### J10 — Both temporal directions remain open

New evidence may revise the past representation; new capability may reopen the future.

### J11 — Foresight becomes answerable

### J12 — Escalate embodiment only when needed

### J13 — Stronger embodiment requires stronger care

Real stakes increase, rather than decrease, governance obligations.

### J14 — Gate promotion, not possibility

Failure to promote a branch does not erase it from The Possible.

### J15 — Synthesis and governance remain distinct

Janus guards transitions around the Spirit of Synthesis; it does not replace synthesis itself.

## 37. Working definition

> **Janus is the bidirectional, non-monotonic governor of the thresholds surrounding the Spirit of Synthesis. Janus.Future qualifies how Cognitive Packets and possibilities may advance toward incarnation; Janus.Past qualifies how realized consequences and traces may be assimilated into revisable knowledge. Janus preserves competing alternatives, unresolved residue and revision conditions, consumes rather than monopolizes specialized assessments, and promotes prospective tests toward the least sufficient degree of embodiment required for Reality to discriminate them. Janus qualifies transitions; it does not manufacture Reality, synthesis or authority.**

Short form:

> **Janus guards the passage from The Possible toward incarnation, and the return from The Realized toward knowledge.**

Operational compression:

> **Janus.Future makes commitments. Janus.Past makes them answerable.**

Embodiment compression:

> **Use as little Reality as sufficient — but enough Reality to let Reality answer.**
